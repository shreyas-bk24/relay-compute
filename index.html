How Relay works

Owners stay in control of their machines. Jobs only run when a machine is free, and they never touch the owner's files.

  1. Owners set a schedule

    A lightweight Rust agent detects the GPU and only offers the machine during the hours its owner chooses, like overnight or during the workday.

  2. The coordinator matches jobs

    A coordinator keeps a live gRPC connection to every node and assigns each job to a free machine with enough GPU memory.

  3. Jobs run in a sandbox

    Each job runs isolated from the owner's files. Multi-step work hands off between nodes through object storage, and results go back to the team.

Built for work that doesn't need a datacenter on standby

Consumer GPUs with 8 to 32 GB of memory handle a lot of real AI work, as long as the job can tolerate a machine going away.

A good fit today

  • LoRA and QLoRA fine-tuning7B to 13B models fit on a single 24 GB card.
  • Evaluations and benchmarksMany independent runs spread across nodes.
  • Embedding generationLarge batch jobs that split cleanly.
  • Synthetic data and batch inferenceOpen models, run in bulk, results collected later.

Not a fit yet

  • Real-time, latency-sensitive inference
  • Training that needs many tightly linked GPUs
  • Models too large for one consumer card, until multi-GPU splitting ships

Questions

Can a job read my files?

No. Jobs run in a sandbox with no access to your files, and the agent only offers your machine during the hours you allow.

What happens if I need my PC back mid-job?

Your use comes first. We're building checkpoint-based failover so an interrupted job resumes on another machine instead of starting over.

Which GPUs can join?

Modern NVIDIA consumer cards. Cards with 24 to 32 GB, like the RTX 4090 and 5090, can take fine-tuning jobs; smaller cards handle embeddings and lighter inference.

Is this live?

Not publicly. The core job loop works on real hardware, and we're onboarding a small group of teams and owners first.
